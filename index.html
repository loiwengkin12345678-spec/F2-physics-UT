Astrophysics, Stellar Evolution, Gravity, Waves & Complete Practice Assessments

1. Astrophysics & Stellar Evolution

  • Our Location: Solar system is located in the Milky Way galaxy[cite: 3, 9]. A large collection of stars is called a galaxy[cite: 3, 9].
  • Stellar Life Cycle (Medium Star): Nebula $\rightarrow$ Protostar $\rightarrow$ Main Sequence $\rightarrow$ Red Giant $\rightarrow$ White Dwarf[cite: 3, 6, 9].
  • Stellar Life Cycle (Massive Star): Nebula $\rightarrow$ Protostar $\rightarrow$ Main Sequence $\rightarrow$ Red Supergiant $\rightarrow$ Supernova $\rightarrow$ Neutron Star or Black Hole[cite: 3, 6, 9].
  • H-R Diagram: Vertical axis represents absolute magnitude/luminosity; horizontal axis represents decreasing temperature[cite: 6, 7].
  • Cosmology: Red-shift indicates distant galaxies are moving away[cite: 3, 9]. Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation supports the Big Bang theory[cite: 3, 9].

2. Gravity & Orbits

  • Planets Order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (from the Sun outwards)[cite: 9].
  • Comets: Made of ice and dust, highly elliptical orbits, travel fastest when closest to the Sun due to stronger gravitational pull[cite: 3, 9].
  • Centripetal Force: Force acting towards the center for circular motion (provided by gravity for orbits)[cite: 3, 9].
  • Gravitational Field Strength: Determined by both the mass of the body and the distance from it[cite: 3].

3. Wave Basics & Calculations

  • Transverse vs Longitudinal: Transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to wave direction; longitudinal waves vibrate parallel (features compressions & rarefactions)[cite: 4].
  • Wave Parameters: Amplitude (max displacement), Wavelength ($\lambda$), Frequency ($f$, oscillations per second in Hz)[cite: 4, 8].
  • Formulas:
    $v = f \times \lambda$ (Wave Speed = Frequency × Wavelength)[cite: 4, 8]
    $f = \frac{\text{Number of Oscillations}}{\text{Time}}$[cite: 10]  |  $T = \frac{1}{f}$[cite: 4, 8]
    $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$ (Orbital Speed)[cite: 5]

Short & Long Answer Questions (6 Questions)

Form 3 analytical and calculation practice questions.

1. Describe the life cycle of a massive star like Betelgeuse, beginning from a cloud of dust and gas.[cite: 3]
Answer:
1. Starts as a nebula (cloud of dust and gas); gravity pulls the gas and dust together to form a protostar[cite: 3].
2. Nuclear fusion begins in the core, turning it into a main sequence star[cite: 3].
3. When core hydrogen runs out, the star expands to become a red supergiant[cite: 3].
4. Eventually, the core collapses rapidly in a massive supernova explosion[cite: 3].
5. After the supernova, the remaining dense core forms a neutron star, or a black hole if the mass is extremely high[cite: 3].
2. Explain why a comet's speed will be greatest when it is closest to the star in its elliptical orbit.[cite: 5]
Answer:
When the comet is closest to the star, the gravitational pull from the star is the strongest[cite: 5]. This strong gravitational force accelerates the comet, causing its orbital speed to be at its maximum.
3. A wave in a pond, travelling at $0.5\text{ m/s}$, makes a floating ball move up and down twice every second ($2\text{ Hz}$). Calculate the frequency and wavelength of the wave.[cite: 4]
Answer:
- Frequency ($f$) = 2 Hz (since it moves up and down twice per second)[cite: 4].
- Using the wave speed equation: $v = f \times \lambda$[cite: 4, 8]
- $0.5 = 2 \times \lambda$[cite: 4]
- $\lambda = \frac{0.5}{2} = \mathbf{0.25\text{ m}}$[cite: 4].
4. Explain what red-shift indicates about the movement of distant galaxies and how it provides evidence for the origin of the universe.[cite: 3, 9]
Answer:
Red-shift indicates that distant galaxies are moving away from us[cite: 3, 9], and the farther away they are, the faster they are moving. This shows that the universe is expanding, which supports the Big Bang model (where everything originated from a single hot, dense point).
5. State the main differences between transverse and longitudinal waves, and give one example of each type.[cite: 4]
Answer:
- **Transverse Waves:** Particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel (Example: Light waves or water ripples)[cite: 4].
- **Longitudinal Waves:** Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel, creating compressions and rarefactions (Example: Sound waves)[cite: 4].
6. Calculation: Object B has an orbital radius of $42,000\text{ km}$ and a time period of $24\text{ hours}$. Calculate the orbital speed of object B in $\text{km/s}$ using scientific notation or standard form.[cite: 5]
Answer:
Formula: $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$[cite: 5]
- $r = 42,000\text{ km} = 42,000,000\text{ m}$ (or keep in km and convert time to seconds)
- Time $T = 24\text{ hours} = 24 \times 3600 = 86,400\text{ seconds}$
- $v = \frac{2 \times 3.14 \times 42,000,000}{86,400} \approx \mathbf{3,052.78\text{ m/s}}$ (or $3.05 \times 10^3\text{ m/s}$)[cite: 5].
